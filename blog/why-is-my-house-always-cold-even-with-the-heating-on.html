Why Is My House Always Cold Even With the Heating On? 9 Causes and Fixes

CozyAtNight · 28 October 2026 · General guidance only — not heating-engineering advice. Boiler, gas and structural work belongs to qualified professionals.

If you keep asking why is my house always cold even with the heating on, you are not imagining it — and turning the thermostat up is usually the most expensive way to respond. When a heating system runs for hours yet rooms stay chilly, the heat is being made but lost, blocked, misdirected or mismeasured. Work through the nine causes below in order — each one is common, most are cheap to fix, and together they explain the great majority of cold houses.

Before you start, one measurement matters: a simple thermometer in the cold room. Feelings mislead; a room at a steady, genuinely low temperature points to heat loss or under-delivery, while a room at a reasonable temperature that still feels cold points to draughts, cold floors and cold surfaces — a different set of fixes.

1 and 2: radiators that are not delivering — air and sludge

Feel each radiator while the system runs. Cold at the top, warm at the bottom means trapped air is occupying the space the hot water should fill — the fix is bleeding, covered step by step in our guide to how to bleed a radiator cold at the top. Cold at the bottom or in the middle, warm at the top suggests sludge — corrosion debris settling where flow is slowest — which needs a heating engineer's power flush or chemical clean, not a DIY fix. One radiator cold everywhere while others are hot usually means a closed or stuck valve, or a system that needs balancing so each radiator gets its fair share of flow; valves you can check yourself, balancing you can attempt carefully or hand to an engineer.

3: the thermostat is lying about your rooms

The thermostat measures the temperature where it sits — nowhere else. If it sits in a warm hallway near a radiator, it can switch the whole system off while the living room is still cold. If it sits in a draught or on a cold outside wall, it does the opposite. Check what is near yours: a radiator with its valve wide open beside the thermostat is a classic mismatch. Radiator thermostatic valves, where fitted, let you hold individual rooms at their own settings — but the room with the main thermostat should normally have its radiator valves open, so the thermostat stays in charge. Portable room thermometers in the rooms you actually sit in tell you the truth the hallway never will.

4 and 5: draughts in, and heat straight out

A room can be heated to a fair temperature and still feel cold if air keeps moving through it: moving air strips warmth from skin, and every leak is also heat escaping. The usual culprits are door gaps, window frames, letterboxes, keyholes, skirting joins and floorboard gaps. Find them properly before sealing anything — our guide to how to find draughts in your home uses hand, tissue and incense tests — then fix the biggest first with the cheap materials in draught fixes under $25. Above you, an uninsulated or thinly insulated loft lets heat rise out of the house all day; you cannot fix that with tape, but knowing it is the cause stops you blaming the boiler.

6 and 7: cold floors and cold walls

Rooms feel cold when their surfaces are cold, even at a decent air temperature. Bare floorboards over a ventilated void, tiles on an uninsulated slab, and single-skinned outside walls all radiate chill. Floors respond to rugs, underlay and sealed gaps — the full cheap programme is in our guide to how to insulate cold floors cheap. Outside walls respond to being kept dry and, where a radiator sits on one, to reflector foil behind it, assessed honestly in radiator reflector foil: does it work?. Single-glazed windows chill the air beside them into a falling cold curtain; heavy curtains closed at dusk, or secondary film, blunt that effect — see do thermal curtains really work for what curtains can and cannot do.

8: furniture and curtains blocking the heat

A sofa pushed against a radiator, or a long curtain tucked behind it, sends the heat up the wall and out of the glass instead of into the room. Radiators need air moving freely across them: pull furniture a hand's width clear, keep curtains in front of — not behind — the radiator or lift them onto the sill, and never dry clothes over one as a habit; the heat goes into the laundry and the moisture into the room. This cause costs nothing to fix and is embarrassingly common.

9: the system itself — sized, serviced or scheduled wrong

Sometimes the house is fine and the system is the cause. A boiler or furnace that has not been serviced can run inefficiently; a heating schedule set for a household that has changed — rooms heated empty all day while evenings feel short of heat — wastes its output where nobody sits. And some homes, especially extended or converted ones, simply have radiators too small for the rooms they serve. Those are engineer questions: ask for the system to be checked, balanced and serviced, and for radiator sizing to be assessed in the cold room, before you accept "turn it up" as the answer. The habits in our guide to keeping a house warm without turning the heating up will stretch whatever output you have in the meantime.

Why is my house always cold even with the heating on? The order to work in

Cheapest and most likely first: check radiators room by room (air, valves, blockages), check the thermostat's location and the thermometer truth, hunt and seal the big draughts, warm the floors and window walls, and only then spend money on the system itself. Each step either fixes the problem or removes a suspect — which is exactly what a good diagnosis is for.

A note on expectations: what warm enough actually means

One last check is against your own expectations. Comfort guidance for living rooms generally centres on a comfortable, steady warmth rather than tropical heat, and bedrooms are usually kept cooler for sleep — our guide to the best bedroom temperature for sleep in winter explains why. If your thermometer shows the living room holding a steady, reasonable temperature yet one person still feels cold, look again at where they sit: beside the single-glazed window, over the bare floor, in the draught path between two doors. Moving a chair two metres is occasionally the cheapest heating upgrade in the house.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my house always cold even with the heating on?

Usually because heat is being lost, blocked or mismeasured rather than not made: air or sludge in radiators, a thermostat in a warm spot switching the system off early, draughts stripping warmth, cold floors and walls, furniture blocking radiators, or a system that needs servicing or balancing. Work through the causes in order, cheapest first.

Why is one room always colder than the rest of the house?

Start with that room's radiator: cold at the top means air to bleed out, cold at the bottom suggests sludge, and cold all over points to a valve or balancing problem. Then check the room's outside walls, windows and floor — the room with the most external surface and the biggest draughts will always lag behind.

Can a thermostat in the wrong place make the house feel cold?

Yes. The thermostat only measures its own spot. If it sits in a warm hallway or near a radiator, it can switch the heating off while living rooms are still cold. A thermometer in the room you sit in shows the real temperature, and radiator valves in other rooms can balance things out.

Why does my house feel cold even at a normal temperature?

Because comfort depends on surfaces and air movement, not just air temperature. Draughts strip warmth from skin, and cold floors, walls and window glass radiate chill. Sealing leaks, adding rugs and closing heavy curtains at dusk can make the same measured temperature feel noticeably warmer.

When should I call a heating engineer about a cold house?

When radiators stay patchy after bleeding, when one radiator never heats, when the boiler makes new noises or loses pressure repeatedly, or when the system has not been serviced for a long time. Sludge, balancing and sizing problems are engineer work — and gas appliances should only ever be handled by qualified professionals.
