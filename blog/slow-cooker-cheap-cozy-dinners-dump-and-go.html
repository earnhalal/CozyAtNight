Slow Cooker Cheap Cozy Dinners, Dump and Go: Cozy Food for Pennies Per Hour

CozyAtNight · 28 October 2026 · General information only — running costs use illustrative example rates and an example wattage; food-safety notes follow commonly cited guidance.

If you searched for slow cooker cheap cozy dinners dump and go, you already know the promise: ingredients in the pot in the morning, dinner ready when you walk in. It is one of the few kitchen promises that is basically true. A slow cooker asks for ten minutes of assembly, no skill beyond chopping, and then quietly turns the cheapest ingredients in the shop — tough cuts, root vegetables, dried beans — into the kind of food that makes a house smell like somebody's grandmother is in charge. This guide gives three templates that follow the method, works out honestly what an afternoon of slow cooking costs to run, and covers the safety rules that are not optional.

What a slow cooker actually costs to run

This is the crossover this site cannot resist, because slow cookers are the dinner version of our favourite subject: cost per hour. The formula is the same one we use for heaters — kilowatts × hours × your price per kilowatt-hour. A slow cooker is a small appliance: labels vary by size and setting, and plenty of mid-size models draw somewhere in the region of 200W on their low setting. Take that as an illustrative figure and check the label on yours. At 200W, an eight-hour cook uses 0.2 × 8 = 1.6 kWh in total. At an illustrative US rate of 16¢ per kWh, that is about 26¢ for the whole cook; at an illustrative UK rate of 24p per kWh, about 38p. Under five pence or a few cents an hour — genuinely pennies per hour, as promised.

The honest comparison with an oven needs one caveat. An oven draws far more per hour — typically a few kilowatts — but finishes most dishes far faster. For the long, slow braises a slow cooker exists for, the cooker generally wins on total energy as well as on convenience; and unlike the oven in the guide to the cheapest way to heat a room at night, nobody is suggesting you heat the house with it. It simply cooks dinner at a lower hourly draw than almost anything else in the kitchen, while asking nothing of you in between.

Slow cooker cheap cozy dinners: dump and go templates that work

Every template follows the same skeleton: hard vegetables at the bottom where the heat and liquid are, protein and pulses in the middle, liquid to about two-thirds, lid on, walk away. Amounts suit a medium cooker feeding four, or two with leftovers worth having.

1. Chicken, potatoes and root vegetables

Into the pot: four to six chicken thighs (thawed, skin removed if you like), four potatoes in big chunks, three carrots in thick slices, an onion in wedges, two cloves of garlic, a teaspoon of dried thyme or mixed herbs, and 300ml hot stock poured over. Cook on low for 6 to 8 hours. Near the end, stir in a handful of frozen peas and season properly. If you want gravy rather than broth, lift out the chicken and vegetables, and simmer the liquid in a pan with a spoon of cornflour mixed in cold water until it thickens.

2. Bean and vegetable chilli

Into the pot: two tins of beans (kidney, black, or one of each), drained; a tin of chopped tomatoes; an onion, a pepper and two carrots, all diced; two cloves of garlic; a tablespoon of tomato purée; a heaped teaspoon each of smoked paprika and ground cumin, and chilli to your courage. Add 200ml stock. Cook on low for 6 to 8 hours. Serve over rice or with bread. This one is even better the next day, and it freezes like a dream — it is a close cousin of the batch-cooked soups elsewhere on this site.

3. Beef and barley stew

Into the pot: about 600g of a stewing cut of beef in chunks (thawed), two onions, three carrots, two sticks of celery if you have them, 150g pearl barley rinsed, two cloves of garlic, a bay leaf if the cupboard provides, and 700ml hot stock. Cook on low for 8 hours, until the beef yields to a spoon and the barley is tender and has thickened everything around it. Season at the end. This is the weekend one — the smell alone earns its place.

The food-safety rules that are not optional

Slow cooking is gentle cooking, and gentle means the safety steps matter. Thaw meat and poultry fully in the fridge first — standard food-safety guidance is clear that frozen meat can linger too long at unsafe temperatures in a slowly warming pot. Fill the pot between half and two-thirds full: too little and dinner overcooks and dries; too much and it heats too slowly and may overflow. Keep the lid on — every peek lets out accumulated heat and adds cooking time; the glass lid exists so you can look without lifting. Vegetables go at the bottom, in the liquid, because they cook slowest. And when dinner is done, treat leftovers like any other cooked food: cool them and get them into the fridge promptly — commonly cited guidance says within about two hours — reheat them until steaming hot throughout, and reheat each portion once.

When dump-and-go disappoints, and the fix

Three complaints account for nearly every slow-cooker disappointment. Watery results: a sealed pot loses almost no liquid, so use less than you would on the stove — the templates above already do — and finish with the lid off on high for the last half hour if a sauce needs reducing. Blandness: long cooking mutes seasonings, so season properly at the end, and wake the pot up with the same bright finishes as soup — a squeeze of lemon, fresh herbs, a spoon of vinegar in a stew works wonders. Mush: delicate vegetables and pasta do not belong in an eight-hour cook; add peas, greens and small pasta near the end, or cook pasta separately. None of these are the cooker's fault, and all of them are fixed by the second attempt.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a slow cooker cost to run for 8 hours?

Use the formula: kilowatts × hours × your price per kilowatt-hour. A slow cooker drawing an illustrative 200W on low uses 0.2 × 8 = 1.6 kWh over eight hours — about 26¢ at an example US rate of 16¢ per kWh, or about 38p at an example UK rate of 24p. Your own figure depends on your cooker's label wattage and your tariff, so check both.

Can I leave a slow cooker on while I am out?

Slow cookers are designed to cook unattended for hours, and many households use them exactly that way. The sensible precautions are the manufacturer's own: place it on a stable, heatproof surface away from anything flammable, keep the cord safe, fill it correctly, and make sure it is in good working order. If leaving any heated appliance running worries you, cook while you are home instead — the food is the same.

Can you put frozen meat in a slow cooker?

Standard food-safety guidance says no: thaw meat and poultry fully in the fridge before it goes in the pot. A slow cooker heats up gradually, and frozen meat can spend too long in the temperature zone where bacteria multiply before it ever gets hot. Thawing overnight in the fridge adds no morning effort — it just moves the step to the night before.

Why are dump-and-go dinners so cheap to make?

Because the method suits cheap ingredients and wastes nothing. Slow, moist cooking tenderises inexpensive cuts that would be tough cooked quickly, turns dried beans and lentils into dinner without fuss, and cooks everything in one pot, so the cooking liquid — and everything in it — becomes the sauce rather than being thrown away.

Do slow cookers use less electricity than an oven?

Usually, for the jobs they share — but the comparison needs care. An oven draws far more per hour, often a few kilowatts, while a slow cooker draws a few hundred watts; but the oven finishes most dishes in a fraction of the time. For long, slow braises the slow cooker generally wins on total energy. For a twenty-minute bake, the oven was never really in a contest.
