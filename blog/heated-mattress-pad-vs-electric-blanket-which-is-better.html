Heated Mattress Pad vs Electric Blanket Which Is Better (and Cheaper to Run)?

CozyAtNight · 28 October 2026 · General guidance only. Energy prices vary by country, provider and tariff — every rate below is clearly labelled as illustrative, and we show you how to calculate your own.

If you are asking heated mattress pad vs electric blanket which is better, you are really asking where the heat should start. In the US, a heated mattress pad is the name for what many other countries call an electric underblanket: a fitted heated layer that goes on the mattress, under you. An electric blanket, in this comparison, goes over you. Both plug in, both use far less power than heating a room, and both can make a cold bed welcoming. They simply deliver warmth in opposite directions.

Heated mattress pad vs electric blanket which is better — the short answer

For warming the bed itself, the pad usually has the edge: its heat starts below you, rises through you, and is held in by the duvet above, so less escapes into the room. For warmth you can pull over your shoulders or remove as the night changes, the blanket is more flexible. On cost there is no automatic winner — both sit in roughly the same 50–150W class, so the wattage, setting and hours you actually use matter more than the category.

Under you vs over you: how each one works

A heated mattress pad fits over the mattress like a fitted sheet, with heating elements spread through it. You sleep on top of it, usually with your normal sheet above it if the maker's instructions say so. Switch it on before bed and it warms the mattress and sheet first, so that icy first contact underneath you is already gone when you get in.

An electric blanket works from the other side. It lies over you, warming the space between you and the top bedding, and you feel it first on your front, shoulders and legs. You can fold it back or push it down in a way you cannot with a fitted pad. A bunched blanket no longer spreads heat evenly, and it should never be left folded or bunched while on.

Why heat rising gives the pad its edge

Heat from a pad travels up through the mattress, sheet and you, then meets the duvet, which slows it down. The bed becomes a small insulated pocket — warmth made at the bottom, trapped at the top — which is why a pad on a modest setting can feel evenly warm, and why pre-heating works: the mattress holds warmth before you arrive.

Heat from an over-blanket also rises, but rising takes it away from you: warmth escapes into the room air unless a duvet holds it down. So a blanket can feel wonderfully warm at first touch, yet need a higher setting on a very cold night. The pad tends to win on efficiency; the blanket, on immediate cosiness.

Typical wattages and what each one costs to run

Most heated mattress pads and electric blankets sit in roughly the same power range: about 50–150W depending on size, setting and whether two controllers are running. Read your own label, because the formula only works with a real number. For wider context, our guide to electric blanket vs space heater shows why either heated bedding option costs so much less than heating room air.

The formula never changes: cost per hour = kilowatts × your price per kWh. Divide watts by 1,000 to get kilowatts: 60W is 0.06 kW, 100W is 0.1 kW, 150W is 0.15 kW.

Worked example — illustrative rates. The primary illustrative rate here is 16 US cents per kWh, an example US residential rate, because mattress pad is mainly a US term. As a note, we also show an illustrative UK rate of 24p per kWh. Neither is your tariff — use the price per kWh on your own bill in the same formula.

At the US example: a 60W pad costs 0.06 × 16¢ = about 0.96 cents per hour. A 100W model costs 0.1 × 16¢ = 1.6 cents per hour. A 150W model costs 0.15 × 16¢ = 2.4 cents per hour. At the illustrative UK rate the same sums are about 1.44p, 2.4p and 3.6p per hour. Note: thermostats cycle to hold a setting, so a night on low usually costs less than maximum wattage × every hour.

Feel and bedding layers

A pad makes the bed itself feel warm — warm sheet, warm mattress, no cold patch when you roll over — while the top stays familiar: sheet, you, then your usual duvet. What it cannot do is be pulled up around your shoulders, and it stays part of the made bed.

An electric blanket changes the feel above you: under a duvet it warms the whole top layer, and on its own it feels like being covered by something already warm — hard to beat for reading in bed. It suits restless sleepers less well, and for the sofa neither product is right; a heated throw is designed for that job.

Dual controls: the feature couples should check first

If one partner wants more heat, dual controls matter more than a small wattage difference. On many larger pads and some larger blankets, each side has its own controller, heat zone and often timer, so one person can run warm while the other runs cooler or off — saving energy too. Check that dual means two independent zones and that each side has its own auto shut-off. Our guide on how to choose an electric blanket covers controls and timers in depth, and most of it applies to pads too.

Washing and care: follow the label

Heated bedding is washable only in the way its maker says, and models differ — treat your care label and instruction booklet as the authority, not any general guide. In general terms, controllers and cables are disconnected and removed before washing, the product is checked for damage first, and it must be completely dry before it is reconnected or switched on. A pad has one practical advantage: it lives under the sheet, and the sheet takes most everyday contact, so it usually needs washing less often than a blanket that sits over you.

Safety notes that apply to both

Never use either product folded, bunched, creased or trapped while switched on, because concentrated heat in one spot damages elements and is exactly what the instructions warn against. Never stack them together: do not use a heated mattress pad and an electric blanket in the same bed at the same time. Pick one heated layer and let plain bedding do the rest. Keep controls and cables where they will not be trapped or stood on.

Follow your maker's instructions on timers, suitable use and who should not use heated bedding — keep to that guidance rather than general advice, especially where the instructions name people who should not use the product or should use it only with supervision. Inspect each product at the start of every season, and if it is old, damaged or behaving oddly — uneven heat, smells, scorching — switch it off and replace it.

Frequently asked questions

Is a heated mattress pad cheaper to run than an electric blanket?

At the same wattage they cost the same, because cost is kilowatts multiplied by your price per kWh. A pad often feels warm on a lower setting, though, because its heat rises through you and is held in by the bedding above. A 100W model of either type costs about 1.6 cents per hour at an illustrative US rate of 16 cents per kWh.

Can you use a heated mattress pad and an electric blanket together?

No. Do not stack a heated mattress pad and an electric blanket together in the same bed while either is switched on. Two heated layers can trap too much heat in one place. Choose one heated layer, use plain unheated bedding with it as its instructions allow, and follow the maker's guidance.

Which feels better, a heated mattress pad or an electric blanket?

A pad warms the bed from underneath, so the mattress and sheet feel warm when you first get in. A blanket warms from above, so your top, shoulders and legs feel it first. People who hate climbing into a cold bed usually prefer the pad; people who like warmth pulled over them often prefer the blanket.

Can couples use different temperatures on each side?

Often, yes. Many larger heated mattress pads and some larger electric blankets offer dual controls, with a separate controller and heat zone for each side. One partner can run warmer, cooler or off while the other chooses differently. Smaller sizes usually have one control for the whole surface, so check before buying.

How do you wash a heated mattress pad or electric blanket?

Follow the care label on your exact model first, because instructions vary. In general, disconnect and remove the controller and cables before washing, and make sure the product is completely dry and inspected before reconnecting it. Never use a heated product that is damaged, still damp, or folded and bunched while it is on.
