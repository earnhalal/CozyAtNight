How to Wash an Electric Blanket Safely (Without Ruining the Wiring)

CozyAtNight · 28 October 2026 · General guidance only. Your blanket's care label and instruction booklet are the authority for your model — if anything here conflicts with them, follow the label.

Learning how to wash an electric blanket safely starts with one rule that overrides everything else in this guide: the care label on your exact blanket is the authority, and it comes first. Models differ in fabric, wiring and connectors, so read the label and instruction booklet before you run any water. If the label says hand wash only, do not machine wash; if it says do not wash, do not wash it. The steps below follow the careful routine most washable models share.

Before any water: disconnect, then inspect

Switch the blanket off, unplug it from the wall, and disconnect the controller and lead from the blanket itself before washing. Follow the maker's instructions for how the connector releases — never tug the cord to pull it free. The controller and mains lead never go in the water or the washing machine. Set them aside somewhere dry.

Next, spread the blanket flat and inspect it slowly, in good light. Look and feel along its full surface for exposed or kinked wiring, scorch or melt marks, fraying, holes, cracked connectors, a damaged cord, or a controller that is cracked or discoloured. If you find damage, stop here: never wash a damaged blanket, and never use one either. Leave it disconnected and follow the maker's guidance on replacement.

How to wash an electric blanket safely, step by step

Once the label allows washing and the blanket has passed inspection, the routine is deliberately unhurried. Shake loose dust off outdoors or over a bath, and avoid soaking connectors. Keep the blanket as flat and evenly spread as you can, so the internal wires are not pulled into sharp folds. Never wring an electric blanket — not after a machine wash, not after a hand wash, not ever. Twisting can kink, stretch or break the fine heating elements inside, and that damage may not be visible afterwards.

Underblanket vs overblanket: what changes when you wash

An electric underblanket — the fitted layer called a heated mattress pad in the US — goes on the mattress under your sheet, so it picks up less direct contact and usually needs washing less often. Remove it from the mattress completely, with straps or skirt freed, before washing. An overblanket goes over you, takes more contact from skin, nightwear and spills, and tends to need washing more often — but never bunch it up in the machine.

Whichever type you own, the safety steps do not change: label first, controller disconnected, inspection passed, no wringing, and completely dry before it goes back on the bed. If you are still choosing, our guide on how to choose an electric blanket covers fit, controls and safety features.

Machine washing, when the label allows it

If — and only if — the care label permits machine washing, use the gentle or delicate cycle, and the water temperature and detergent the maker specifies. Load the blanket on its own, spread evenly around the drum rather than pushed in as a tight ball. If the label allows a brief soak first, keep it brief. Keep the spin as gentle as directed: a long, hard spin stresses wiring much like wringing does, so do not add extra cycles. When the cycle ends, lift the blanket out supporting its weight evenly — dragging a heavy wet blanket by one corner pulls on the elements inside. Reshape it flat straight away; never leave it wet and folded in the drum.

Hand washing, and when it is the better choice

Hand washing suits blankets whose labels say hand wash only, smaller blankets, and any model you want to treat with maximum care. Fill a bath with water at the temperature the label specifies and mix in only the detergent the maker allows. Submerge the blanket, keeping the connector end out of the water if your instructions say to, and gently press the water through the fabric with open hands, working slowly rather than scrubbing. Drain and refill with clean water to rinse, pressing gently until the water runs clear. To remove water, press the blanket against the side of the bath, or roll it loosely in a clean, dry towel and press — supporting it, never twisting it, and never wringing it out over the edge of the bath.

Drying: air-dry fully, and never switch on damp

Drying is where patience matters most. Air-dry the blanket fully: drape it evenly over a clothes airer, a line as the maker allows, or a clean flat surface, with its weight spread so no section hangs in a heavy, waterlogged fold. Re-drape it as needed so the middle layers dry as thoroughly as the edges. Some makers allow a specific tumble-dryer setting — use one only if your label explicitly permits it, exactly as directed, and finish with air-drying regardless.

The damp rule — no exceptions. Never reconnect the controller, never plug in, and never switch on an electric blanket while any part of it is still damp. Cool to the touch is not proof of dryness: thick seams and the areas around the wiring dry last. Give it more time than you think it needs, and only reconnect once it is completely dry throughout and has passed another quick inspection.

Do not iron an electric blanket unless the maker explicitly allows it, and never try to speed drying by switching it on — that is precisely the damp use the rule above forbids. Once it is back in service, our guide asking do electric blankets use a lot of electricity works through running costs.

Dry cleaning, spot cleaning and smells

Dry-clean an electric blanket only if the maker allows it — that means the care label or booklet explicitly permits dry cleaning. Solvents and commercial handling can damage wiring and insulation, so if the label is silent or says do not dry-clean, take that as a no. For a small spill, unplug and disconnect the blanket first, then spot clean only as the label permits, using as little moisture as possible. Afterwards, let the area dry completely and inspect it before reconnecting anything. A washed blanket that still smells scorched or musty should not go back into use — odour can signal damage or trapped moisture.

Storage: loose, dry and uncrushed

Store an electric blanket only when it is completely dry and has passed a careful inspection. Loosely roll it if you can — rolling avoids the sharp creases that repeated tight folding creates — or fold it loosely with soft, wide folds. Keep it in a cool, dry place with nothing heavy on top, because months of weight on one fold can stress the wiring. Keep the controller and cord with it, loosely coiled rather than tightly wound or knotted. A seasonal inspection routine

The start of the cold season is the natural moment to wash, dry and check a stored blanket — and it should be inspected again even if it was clean when it went away.

Frequently asked questions

Can you put an electric blanket in the washing machine?

Only if the care label on your exact blanket says machine washing is allowed. Disconnect and remove the controller first, inspect the blanket, use a gentle cycle exactly as directed, and never wash a damaged blanket. If the label says hand wash only, follow the label.

Can you tumble dry an electric blanket?

Air-drying fully is the safest general approach, and many makers prefer it. Use a tumble dryer only if your care label specifically allows it, on the setting it names. Never reconnect or switch the blanket on while any part of it is still damp.

Can you dry-clean an electric blanket?

Only if the maker allows it. Dry-clean only when the care label or instruction booklet explicitly permits dry cleaning, because solvents and handling can damage wiring and insulation. If the label does not mention it, do not dry-clean the blanket.

What should you do if an electric blanket is damaged?

Do not wash it and do not use it. Unplug it, leave it disconnected, and follow the maker's guidance on repair or replacement. Exposed wires, scorch marks, fraying, a damaged cord or controller, or uneven heating are all reasons to stop using the blanket.

How should you store an electric blanket after washing?

Store it only when it is completely dry and has passed a careful inspection. Loosely roll it or fold it loosely without sharp creases, keep it in a cool, dry place with nothing heavy on top, and keep the controller and cord loosely coiled with it, not tightly wound or trapped.
