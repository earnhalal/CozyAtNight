How Much Does a Space Heater Cost to Run? Per Hour, Day and Month

CozyAtNight · 28 October 2026 · General guidance only. Electricity prices vary widely by state, provider and tariff — every rate below is clearly labelled as illustrative, and we show you how to calculate your own.

If you are asking how much does a space heater cost to run, here is the honest short answer: at an illustrative US rate of 16 cents per kWh, a typical 1,500W heater costs about 24 cents per hour at full power, a smaller 750W model about 12 cents. Run for eight hours a day, that is roughly $1.92 or 96 cents a day — and over a 30-day month, about $57.60 or $28.80. Your own number depends on two things only: the heater's wattage and the price per kWh on your bill.

This guide works the figures per hour, per day and per month, explains why your state changes the answer more than the heater does, and shows when a space heater saves money — and when it quietly adds to the bill. It pairs with our room-by-room comparison of the cheapest way to heat a room at night.

How much does a space heater cost to run? The one-minute formula

Every electric heater, whatever its shape or marketing, is billed the same way: cost per hour = kilowatts × price per kWh. Divide watts by 1,000 to get kilowatts. A 1,500W heater is 1.5 kW, so at 16 cents per kWh it costs 1.5 × 16¢ = 24¢ for each hour at full power. There is no efficiency loophole: a watt of electric heat costs the same from a budget fan heater as from a premium ceramic model, because both convert electricity to heat at effectively the same rate.

Read the table as a ceiling. It assumes full power every minute. A heater with a thermostat cycles on and off once the room reaches its setting, so a real evening in a reasonably insulated room usually costs less — how much less depends on the room, the weather and how warm you ask for.

Why your state changes the answer more than the heater

Two households can run the identical 1,500W heater for the identical evening and pay very different amounts, purely because of where they live. At 10 cents per kWh the hour costs 15 cents; at 40 cents it costs 60 cents — four times as much for exactly the same warmth. That is why any single national figure for space heater costs should be treated as a starting point, not your answer.

State is not the only variable. Some tariffs charge more at peak hours, some homes are on time-of-use plans, and delivery charges sit on top of the headline rate. The per-kWh line on your most recent bill, delivery included if it is billed per unit, is the number to use. If your bill shows a blended rate, that is close enough for decisions like these.

When a space heater saves money — and when it does not

A space heater saves money in exactly one situation: when it lets you turn the main heating down. Warming one occupied room to comfort while the rest of the house sits cooler can cost less than heating every room through a furnace or boiler — especially in the evening, when the household is really only using one or two rooms. Our guide to keeping a house warm without turning the heating up covers the zoning habits that make this work.

It does not save money when it is added on top of a house already heated to the same temperature. Then the heater is pure extra cost. It also loses its advantage in a large, draughty room, where it runs at full power for hours and still cannot hold the temperature — fixing the draughts first, with a heater used second, is the cheaper order. And for sleep, weigh the alternatives honestly: our comparison of electric blanket vs space heater running costs shows why warming the bed usually beats warming the bedroom air all night.

Small heater or big heater: which actually costs less?

A smaller heater looks cheaper per hour, but the honest comparison is cost to keep the room comfortable. A 750W heater in a room that needs 1,500W of heat runs flat out and still leaves you cold; a 1,500W heater on a thermostat may cycle at half power and average about the same — while actually warming you. Match the size to the room, then let the thermostat do the saving. Our guide on how to choose a space heater covers sizing, tip-over and overheat protection, and the placement rules that keep one safe.

There is also a style difference worth knowing. Fan heaters warm air fast and stop the moment they switch off. Oil filled radiators are slower but keep releasing stored heat; their costs are worked out in our guide to how much an oil filled radiator costs to run per hour. At the same wattage the hourly ceiling is identical — behaviour, not magic, is the difference.

The safety rules that are part of the real cost

A heater used carelessly can cost far more than electricity. Keep it on a flat, hard floor, well clear of bedding, curtains, furniture and anything that could fall onto it. Plug it directly into a wall outlet — never an extension cord or power strip, which are not built for a sustained heavy load. Choose a model with tip-over and overheat protection, and never leave one running unattended or while you sleep unless the manufacturer's instructions explicitly allow the use you have in mind. Keep children and pets supervised around it, and keep it out of bathrooms unless it is specifically rated for bathroom use.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a space heater cost to run per hour?

At an illustrative US rate of 16 cents per kWh, a 1,500W space heater costs about 24 cents per hour at full power, a 1,000W model about 16 cents and a 750W model about 12 cents. Work out your own figure by dividing the wattage by 1,000 and multiplying by the price per kWh on your bill.

How much does it cost to run a space heater all day?

Running a 1,500W heater for eight hours at 16 cents per kWh costs about $1.92 for the day at full power. Over 30 days at eight hours a day that is roughly $57.60. A thermostat that cycles once the room is warm usually brings the real total below these full-power figures.

Is it cheaper to run a space heater or central heating?

It depends on how much of the house you can let stay cooler. A space heater heating one occupied room, with the main thermostat turned down, can cost less than heating the whole house. Run on top of full central heating, it only adds cost. The saving comes from the setback, not from the heater itself.

Do some space heaters use less electricity than others?

Only by being smaller or cycling more. At the same wattage, every electric heater costs the same per hour, because electricity is billed by the kilowatt-hour and all electric heaters convert it to heat at effectively the same rate. Claims of special efficiency at the same wattage should be treated with suspicion.

Why is my space heater cost higher than the examples here?

Most likely your price per kWh is higher than the illustrative 16 cents used here. US rates vary widely by state, from under 10 cents to over 40 cents, and time-of-use tariffs add peak pricing. Multiply your heater's kilowatts by the rate on your own bill for your real figure.
